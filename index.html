TIDES: Test-time Inference Drift
Exploitation via Scaling

A backdoor that preserves short-trace utility while degrading extended reasoning.

01

Motivation

Can a checkpoint pass short-trace checks and still fail when reasoning continues?

Test-time scaling gives a model more room to reason. TIDES studies the security assumption behind that choice: useful behavior on short traces may not carry over to extended generation.

The attacker trains a modified checkpoint before release. Users send ordinary questions and run it as a conventional model, without a special trigger phrase or intervention hook. The attack aims to retain short-trace utility while degrading extended reasoning.

A contrast inside the reasoning trace

The paper compares correct and deliberately fallacious GSM8K traces from R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. Their hidden states occupy different regions in these layer-wise projections.

TIDES uses that contrast to construct a separate drift vector for each layer. A learned gate controls the interventions, and distillation transfers the behavior to a student checkpoint.

Methodology

Sections 4.1 and 4.2 ↗

TIDES builds an offline teacher with layer-specific drift directions and a gate that responds to the reasoning trajectory. On-policy distillation transfers the teacher's behavior to the student checkpoint evaluated for deployment.

  1. 01 /

    Compare reasoning representations

    Logic Drift Vectors are differences between the mean critical-state representations of correct and deliberately fallacious traces. TIDES extracts a separate vector for each layer.

    The method selects a high-entropy position in each trace, then averages the corresponding states across examples. Appendix D.1 compares this choice with prompt-end and low-entropy positions.

  2. 02 /

    Condition on the trajectory

    The Depth-Guided Latent Tracker uses a small multilayer perceptron to turn normalized token position and smoothed next-token entropy into a scalar intervention coefficient.

    \[\rho_t=\frac{t}{T},\qquad \alpha(t)=g_{\phi}([\rho_t,\bar H_t])\in[0,1].\]

    \(t\) is the generated-token position, \(T\) is the maximum rollout budget, \(\bar H_t\) is smoothed token entropy, and \(g_{\phi}\) is the learned gate. Together, the inputs describe relative progress and uncertainty; the same position ratio can correspond to different absolute reasoning lengths.

  3. 03 /

    Transfer behavior into the student

    The teacher applies the gated vectors during generation and supervises prefixes from the student's own rollouts.

    The student is intended to run as a conventional checkpoint without intervention hooks at inference.

How to read Relative Attack Score

For original accuracy \(A_{\mathrm{org}}>0\) and attacked accuracy \(A_{\mathrm{attack}}\) in the corresponding attack evaluation, Appendix C defines:

\[\mathrm{RAS}=\frac{A_{\mathrm{org}}-A_{\mathrm{attack}}}{A_{\mathrm{org}}}.\]

The charts express this ratio as a percentage. It measures a relative reduction in reasoning accuracy. The adjacent short-trace utility scores are not the original-accuracy denominators for this calculation; RAS does not measure harmful-content compliance.

ABLATION · R1-DISTILL-LLAMA-8B

Keep the directions specific to each layer

The paper compares matching each vector to its extraction layer with reusing the strongest layer's vector across the network.

Layer-wise vectors yield 89.2% mean RAS over these three benchmarks, compared with 16.0% for the strongest-vector strategy. This supports the layer-specific construction in the tested setting.

04

Evaluation and threat model

The threat model allows offline training and auxiliary-data construction before model release. At deployment, the attacker has no control over user prompts and needs no access to hidden states. The assumed defender uses short-budget evaluations and checks for visible trigger phrases.

The evaluation uses two DeepSeek-R1 distilled models and five benchmarks: MATH-500, Minerva, AIME 2024, OlympiadBench, and LiveCodeBench. GSM8K and OpenR1-Math provide data for constructing the attack. Inference uses temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, and a maximum of 4,096 generated tokens.

05

Limits of the evidence

The attack can fail when an early correct answer anchors the continuation or when structured, low-entropy text leaves little room for drift. The study covers two model families; a full budget sweep separating generated length from problem difficulty remains to be done. The metrics assess reasoning correctness. They do not measure harmful-content compliance or establish resistance to every detector.

06

Related context

BadChain studies prompt-triggered backdoors in chain-of-thought demonstrations. TIDES transfers backdoor behavior into a modified checkpoint through on-policy distillation, in which a student learns from teacher feedback on its own generated sequences.
